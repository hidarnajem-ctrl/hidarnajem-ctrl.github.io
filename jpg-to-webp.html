JPG vs WEBP: what is the difference?

JPEG has been the standard photo format for more than 30 years and it works everywhere. WEBP is newer and uses more efficient compression techniques. According to Google's own comparisons, lossy WEBP images are typically 25–34% smaller than JPEG images of comparable quality. For a website with dozens of photos, that difference adds up to noticeably faster loading.

When should you convert JPG to WEBP?

  • Websites and blogs — lighter images improve loading speed and Core Web Vitals such as Largest Contentful Paint.
  • Online stores — product galleries load faster, especially on mobile.
  • Saving storage — keep large photo collections in less space.

Keep JPEG when you need to send a photo to someone using old software, or when a form only accepts JPG files.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free to convert JPG to WEBP?

Yes, completely free with no limits, no watermark and no account.

Will my photo lose EXIF data?

Yes. Like most web optimizers, the converted file does not keep EXIF metadata such as camera model or GPS location — which is also better for privacy.

Can I convert WEBP back to JPG?

Yes. Add a WEBP file and choose JPEG as the output format.
