JPG vs PNG vs WEBP: Which Image Format Should You Use?

By the Teknawy team · November 13, 2026 · 7 min read

Choosing the wrong image format can make a file ten times larger than necessary, or make a sharp logo look blurry. This guide explains how JPG, PNG and WEBP work and when to use each one.

JPG (JPEG)

JPEG was standardised in 1992 and is still the most widely supported image format. It uses lossy compression tuned for photographs: it splits the image into small blocks and stores smooth colour changes very efficiently while discarding detail the eye is unlikely to notice.

Strengths: excellent for photos, supported by every device, camera, app and upload form.
Weaknesses: no transparency; sharp edges and text get fuzzy “ringing” artefacts; quality drops a little each time the file is re-saved.

PNG

PNG uses lossless compression, so the decoded image is identical to the original, pixel for pixel. That makes it ideal for graphics with flat colours, hard edges and text, such as logos, icons, diagrams and screenshots. PNG also supports full transparency.

Strengths: perfect quality, transparency, crisp text.
Weaknesses: photographs saved as PNG are huge — often 5–10× larger than JPEG.

WEBP

WEBP was released by Google in 2010 and is now supported by all major browsers, including Safari since version 14. It offers both lossy and lossless modes, transparency and animation. Google reports that lossy WEBP images are 25–34% smaller than comparable JPEGs, and lossless WEBP images are about 26% smaller than PNGs.

Strengths: the smallest files for both photos and graphics, transparency, wide browser support.
Weaknesses: some older software and certain upload forms still do not accept it.

Which format should you choose?

  • Images for a website or blog: WEBP. Convert with the JPG to WEBP or PNG to WEBP tool.
  • Photos for email, printing or official forms: JPEG, for maximum compatibility.
  • Logos and icons that need perfect edges: PNG or lossless WEBP (or SVG if you have a vector version).
  • Screenshots with text: PNG, or WEBP at high quality (90%+).

What about AVIF?

AVIF is an even newer format that can be smaller than WEBP, and it is supported by current versions of the major browsers. However, encoding is slower and support in editing tools and upload forms is still less common. For most people today, WEBP offers the best balance of size and compatibility.

Conclusion
