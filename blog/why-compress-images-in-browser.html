Why Compressing Images in Your Browser Is Safer

By the Teknawy team · November 13, 2026 · 4 min read

When you use a typical online image compressor, your photo travels across the internet to a company's server, is processed there and is sent back to you. Most services promise to delete files after a while — but you have to trust that promise. There is a better way.

What is client-side compression?

“Client-side” means the work happens on your own device — the “client” — instead of on a remote server. Modern browsers include powerful image tools: they can decode JPG, PNG and WEBP files and re-encode them at a chosen quality using the HTML5 Canvas API. Teknawy uses these built-in features, so your images are processed entirely inside the browser tab.

The benefits

1. Your photos stay private

The image file is never sent anywhere. That makes client-side tools safe for ID documents, contracts, medical paperwork, family photos and confidential screenshots.

2. It is faster

There is no upload and no download of the result. On a slow mobile connection, uploading a 10 MB photo can take longer than compressing it. Locally, compression starts the instant you add the file.

3. It saves mobile data

Server-based tools use your data twice: once to upload the original and once to download the result. Client-side processing uses none for the images themselves.

4. No limits

Because the work happens on your device, there is no need for daily limits, file caps or paid plans to cover server costs.

How can you check that a tool is really client-side?

  • Load the page, then turn off your internet connection (airplane mode) and try compressing an image. A client-side tool keeps working.
  • Advanced users can open the browser's developer tools, go to the Network tab and confirm that no image is uploaded during compression.

Are there any downsides?

Very large images or hundreds of files at once depend on your device's memory, so an older phone may take a little longer. For everyday photos this is rarely noticeable.
