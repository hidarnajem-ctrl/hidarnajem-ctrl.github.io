How JPEG compression works

JPEG is a lossy format: it saves space by discarding fine detail that the human eye barely notices, especially in areas of similar colour. The quality setting controls how much detail is kept. Photos straight from a phone are often saved at a very high quality, so re-saving them at 75–85% usually cuts the size in half or more with no difference you can see on screen.

How to compress JPG files

  1. Drop your JPG/JPEG photos into the box above.
  2. Move the quality slider — 80% is a great starting point.
  3. Compare the before and after sizes shown next to each file.
  4. Download individually or as a ZIP.

Best quality settings for JPEG

  • 90% — printing or archiving important photos.
  • 75–85% — websites, online shops and social media.
  • 60–70% — thumbnails, previews and messaging apps.

Avoid compressing the same JPEG over and over: each save discards a little more detail. Always start from the original file when you need a different size.

Compress and resize together

If your photo is 4000 pixels wide but will be shown at 1200 pixels, resizing it first saves far more space than lowering the quality. Use the resize presets above, or read how to reduce image file size without losing quality.

Frequently asked questions

Does compressing a JPEG reduce its quality?

Technically yes, a little, but at 75% or higher the change is usually invisible on screen.

What is the maximum file size?

There is no fixed limit. Very large images depend only on your device's memory.

Can I keep the original if compression does not help?

Yes. With “Never make a file bigger” enabled, the original file is kept whenever compression would not make it smaller.
